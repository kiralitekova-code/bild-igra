# GDD Structure

Use this reference when creating, auditing, or restructuring a game design document.

## Operating Principles

1. Keep the GDD flexible and updateable.
2. Optimize for what the team or solo creator will actually use.
3. Make headings searchable and stable.
4. Prefer clear decisions, constraints, and open questions over exhaustive prose.
5. Keep implementation tracking lightweight unless the current work needs it.

## Core Sections

1. Executive Summary / Overview
   - One-line pitch
   - Genre, camera, platform, and audience
   - Current project status
   - Design pillars
   - Primary risks or unknowns
2. Scope, Constraints, and Non-Goals
   - Must-have scope
   - Explicit exclusions
   - Engine, platform, schedule, content, input, performance, and team constraints
3. Core Gameplay & Mechanics
   - Core loop
   - Player verbs and controls
   - Objectives, win/loss states, fail states, and scoring
   - Progression, rewards, difficulty curve, economy, and balancing notes
   - Main game elements, systems, enemies, items, levels, or puzzles
4. World & Narrative
   - Setting, tone, premise, characters, factions, and conflict
   - Story progression and player-facing context
   - Lore boundaries that affect gameplay, art, audio, or UI
5. Art & Visual Direction
   - Visual pillars, references, palette, readability goals, and camera framing
   - Character, environment, effects, animation, and feedback direction
   - Asset style constraints and production notes
6. Audio & Music Direction
   - Music mood, pacing, and instrumentation
   - Sound effect language and feedback priorities
   - Voice, ambience, accessibility alternatives, and implementation constraints
7. UI / UX
   - Screen flow, HUD, menus, prompts, onboarding, and feedback
   - Input states, error states, pause/settings, save/load, and accessibility options
   - Readability and player information hierarchy
8. Development Plan & Timeline
   - Milestones, prototype goals, vertical slice, content pass, polish, and release gates
   - Dependencies, risks, owner notes, and current next steps
9. Asset Inventory
   - Required art, animation, audio, UI, narrative, data, and technical assets
   - Status, source, owner, format, size, license, and integration notes where useful
10. Testing & Iteration Plans

- Playtest goals, target scenarios, acceptance criteria, telemetry, and feedback channels
- Known issues, validation commands, runtime checks, and iteration cadence

## Common Gaps

- No clear core loop or player verbs
- Mechanics listed without player goals or feedback
- Art direction without readability constraints
- UI screens listed without player flow
- Asset lists without status or source
- Roadmap items mixed with unresolved design questions
- Accessibility deferred until implementation
- Testing plan limited to "play it" with no acceptance criteria
